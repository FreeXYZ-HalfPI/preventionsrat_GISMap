# Research: how Switzerland and Denmark involve residents and manage safety in cities

Prepared for the Präventionsatlas Buchholz-Kleefeld (developer: MB Saryazdi). Research date: 2 November 2026.
Searches were run in German (Switzerland) and Danish (Denmark). Original terms are in *italics*.

**How to read this:** every point below comes from a search result or fetched page listed under "Sources". Where a source was old, partial or unreadable, that is stated. Nothing here was checked against the original documents beyond what the search tool returned, so treat figures as leads to verify before quoting them publicly.

---

## 1. Switzerland

### 1.1 Direct democracy reaches down to the municipality
- Around 85 % of Swiss municipalities have a *Gemeindeversammlung* (assembly of all eligible voters).
- Optional referendum on municipal decisions. Examples: in the Canton of Aargau, one tenth of eligible voters can force a ballot within 30 days; in the Canton of Zürich, in assembly municipalities, one third of those present can demand a later ballot.
- *Gemeindeinitiative* (municipal initiative): the number of signatures and the collection period vary strongly by municipality.
- *Petition*: non-binding, nobody has to respond. Some councils treat an invalid initiative as a petition.
- A citizens' assembly (*Bürgerrat/Bürgerversammlung*) has at most the legal weight of a petition.

**Relevance for us:** Hannover has no such rights at district level. The Stadtbezirksrat can only be asked (*Anregung*, *Antrag*). A visible, documented route from resident idea to council decision is what we can copy, not the legal power.

### 1.2 Neighbourhood work with paid staff and a legal basis
**Basel-Stadt** (strongest example found)
- Legal basis: § 55 of the cantonal constitution obliges the canton to involve the neighbourhood population when their interests are particularly affected. Implementing ordinance from 2007, participation guide since 2012.
- Structure (*Quartierarbeit*): 16 *Quartiertreffpunkte* (neighbourhood meeting points with fixed opening hours, programme and paid professionals), 2 *Stadtteilsekretariate* (Kleinbasel, Basel-West) plus *Quartierkoordination* Gundeldingen as contact points between residents and administration, 17 *Neutrale Quartiervereine*, and local *Quartierzeitungen*.
- A *Kontaktstelle für Quartierarbeit* coordinates and manages the *Projektkredit Quartierarbeit*. Residents' groups can apply with a form, a short description and a budget (for example street parties or play material), if the canton does not already fund the project.
- Funding scale: the Grand Council considered CHF 5.648 million for 15 meeting points for 2020 to 2023, plus funding for the secretariats.

**Zürich**
- 25 *Quartiervereine* with an umbrella body, the *Quartierkonferenz Zürich*. The city describes them as a mediator between neighbourhood concerns and the administration and supports them with grants calculated by population (2020: between CHF 12,395 and CHF 22,610 per club and year).
- *Quartierarbeit* is mostly done by 17 *Gemeinschaftszentren* (community centres, socio-cultural, paid staff).

**Bern**
- Participation is anchored in the *Gemeindeordnung* (Art. 32). Every district except the inner city has a recognised *Quartierorganisation*.
- Reform project under way: a January 2026 city comparison report, and council motions about *Stadtteilkommissionen* and including residents without voting rights. A 2019 survey showed that participation differs between districts.

**Winterthur**
- Only old or thin results (a 2007 inquiry about *Quartier- und Ortsteilkommissionen*; a *Quartierentwicklungsleitbild* developed with ZHAW and residents). No current evidence found, so no conclusions drawn.

**Relevance for us:** the Präventionsrat is a volunteer body. The Swiss pattern that stands out is a *small, permanent, paid contact point* plus a *small project budget residents can apply for with a one-page form*. This matches the "Nachbarschafts-Oasen" idea in the existing Vienna-based proposal.

### 1.3 School-route safety (*Schulwegsicherheit*)
- *Pedibus*: a walking group for children, accompanied by adults and organised by parents. Well established in French- and Italian-speaking Switzerland, much less in German-speaking Switzerland (VCS school-route study 2026).
- *Schulwegplan*: recommended where routes are objectively dangerous; requires that the municipality is ready to invest.
- Parents' main wishes in the VCS 2026 study: continuous pavements, safe crossings and above all Tempo 30.
- Canton of Zürich: zebra crossings in Tempo-30 zones only with special need to cross, for example at schools (government decision 2025). Winterthur introduced Tempo-30 zones at several school sites, partly with "Achtung Kinder" markings.
- Police checks at school start together with municipal police (Kantonspolizei Zürich, August 2024).

**Relevance for us:** a *Pedibus* or "Laufbus" pilot at one Grundschule is a measure not yet on our school list. The Swiss debate about zebra crossings in Tempo-30 zones is a useful warning: a crossing aid is not always the right tool.

## 2. Denmark

### 2.1 Neighbourhood committees and resident proposals in Copenhagen
- *Lokaludvalg* (local committees): act as consultation partner when law requires public consultation, can comment to outside authorities on matters of special importance for the district, and work on local district planning with the finance administration.
- 2018 to 2019: a broad process with a conference and an "idea factory" on the role and future structure of the local committees. 2022: Vanløse Lokaludvalg was asked to propose a theme for a pilot of a *lokal borgersamling* (local citizens' assembly), planned for autumn 2023. 2015: Kongens Enghave Lokaludvalg asked for experiments with *borgerbudgetter* (participatory budgets).
- *Københavnerforslag* (resident proposals): anyone over 15 with MitID can submit; only residents of the municipality can vote. The original threshold was 5,000 signatures. Press (Altinget, TV 2 Kosmopol) reported a weak start and discussions about lowering the threshold. A skeptical expert warned of backlash if proposals rarely reach City Hall. I could not confirm a current threshold; check kk.dk.

**Relevance for us:** the Copenhagen experience is a lesson in what *not* to do: a high threshold led to few proposals reaching politicians. A low-threshold, visible route (our report map, reviewed monthly) is safer.

### 2.2 Measuring *tryghed* (perceived safety) every year
- Copenhagen has followed residents' perceived safety in their neighbourhoods annually since 2009 (*Tryghedsundersøgelsen*). The 2023 deep-dive analysis was about lighting and safety in the evening and at night, with focus on women.
- Findings reported in the search summary: even lighting without glare generally gives safer public spaces, whereas excessive lighting can work the opposite way. After the city dimmed street lighting in 2022 to save energy, the survey also looked at the effect on residents. Young people name deserted areas without enough light as unsafe.
- Frederiksberg ran its own survey in 2025 (Epinion, more than 2,100 residents). The *Det Kriminalpræventive Råd* (DKR, national Crime Prevention Council) found that almost half of 58 municipalities had recently experienced unease linked to poor lighting.
- I could not open the Copenhagen 2023 lighting report (the link returned 404), so the figures above are second-hand from the search summary.

### 2.3 The *Mørkekort* (darkness map) and *Lys på livet*
- Project "Lys på livet – liv og tryghed gennem nyt lys" in Høje-Taastrup (national ministry, the municipality, lighting specialists and others).
- A light-expert *mørkeanalyse* identified places in housing areas with very poor lighting. It was combined with an anthropological *tryghedsundersøgelse* that mapped what residents perceive as problematic. Result: a *Mørkekort*.
- Key lesson: "more light" is not automatically the solution. Cold, glaring light can feel alienating. The goal is lighting that is crime-preventive, promotes safety and supports social life.
- In Høje-Taastrup a municipal survey found 79 % feel safe and 14 % unsafe.

**Relevance for us:** this is the closest match to our *Beleuchtungs-Schwerpunkte*. Our hotspots are a purely technical map (unlit paths near schools, Kitas, playgrounds, stops). The Danish method adds the missing half: *residents' perception*, collected in the same places, and lighting *quality*, not just presence.

### 2.4 *Tryghedsvandring* (safety walk)
- Part of *Bo Trygt*, a national cooperation of TrygFonden, Realdania, Bolius and DKR. People walk through the area, find the places they feel unsafe, and agree what can be done, for example trimming vegetation or installing light. A Randers example involved the municipality and the housing association, with a follow-up meeting with police.
- DKR research: carrying weapons for self-defence (pepper spray and similar) harms people's sense of safety, whereas residents cooperating to prevent burglaries helps.
- I did not find DKR's own step-by-step method; look at dkr.dk and the Bo Trygt material.

### 2.5 *SSP-samarbejde* (school, social services, police)
- Cooperation in crime prevention for children and young people up to 18, dating back to the 1970s (VIVE gives 1975 as the formal start; Copenhagen sources 1976).
- Structure: a *lokalråd* in each municipality (politicians and officials from school, social services and police) coordinates and sets strategy. A *kredsråd* in each police district (police chief plus the mayors) sits above it. A 2020 national framework allows more actors than the original three.
- VIVE studied the cooperation in 2019 for DKR.

**Relevance for us:** the AG Demokratie school concept and the AG Bildung und Gesundheit could use a lightweight SSP-like round table of school, youth work and police station. In Hannover the nearest equivalent found is *Communities That Care* (see the Hannover/Niedersachsen section on the map).

## 3. Comparison with Hannover

| Question | Switzerland | Denmark | Hannover (today) |
|---|---|---|---|
| How do residents voice ideas? | Assemblies, initiatives, neighbourhood associations, paid contact points | Local committees, resident proposals, annual perception survey | Stadtbezirksrat, Präventionsrat AGs, Mängelhinweiskarte, mobile Sicherheitskonferenz |
| Who is the permanent contact? | Paid staff in meeting points or secretariats (Basel, Zürich) | Municipal staff for local committees; police and municipality in SSP | Stadtbezirksmanagement; volunteers in the AGs |
| Small project money for residents? | Yes (Basel *Projektkredit*) | Participatory-budget experiments proposed | Zuwendungsmittel of the Stadtbezirksrat (not residents' direct tool) |
| Lighting and unease | not found in this research | Annual survey, *Mørkekort*, quality of light | Sicherheitsbefragung 2023 (stops), Sicherheitskonferenz routes |
| School routes | Pedibus, Schulwegplan, Tempo 30 | SSP covers prevention for children, no school-route finding in this research | Schulwegpläne, Elternhaltestelle, Polizei checks |

## 4. Idea candidates for the Präventionsatlas
Ordered by how well they fit what we already have. Effort is a rough guess.

1. **Perception layer ("Wie sicher fühlen Sie sich hier?")**, inspired by the Mørkekort. On the lighting hotspots, residents answer two quick questions (safe or unsafe in the evening? why: too dark, glare, deserted, other). Results shown as a second map layer beside the technical hotspots. Effort: medium.
2. **Safety walk kit (*Tryghedsvandring* / mobile Sicherheitskonferenz)**. A one-page walk route generated from hotspot(s), a checklist and a result form. Effort: low.
3. **Pedibus / Laufbus pilot** as a new measure for primary schools with a main road or many stops nearby. Effort: low (data and text), medium in reality.
4. **Resident micro-grant route** after Basel's *Projektkredit*: a one-page application linked from a map point, reviewed by the AG Wohnen. Effort: low in the map, needs a decision by the Stadtbezirksrat.
5. **SSP-style round table** for the AG Bildung und Gesundheit and AG Demokratie: school, Jugendzentrum, Polizeistation, Stadtbezirksmanagement, twice a year. Not a map feature; belongs in the proposal text.
6. **Lighting quality note**: show hotspots with "type of problem" (dark, glare, deserted) once data exists, so the answer is not always "more lamps".
7. **Annual "Sicherheitsbarometer" for the district**, a short survey repeated yearly, after Copenhagen's *Tryghedsundersøgelse*. Effort: medium; privacy-sensitive.
